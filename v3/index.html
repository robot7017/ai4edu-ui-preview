LabMate AI AR 眼镜

让真实实验，
成为看得见的学习。

学生平板、眼镜与教师工作台协同，让准备、操作与复盘连成完整的学习过程。

看眼镜如何工作认识团队

眼镜为示意渲染；镜片画面来自 BP 原型演示。

要解决的问题

标准流程之外，教师还需要看见过程。

同一间实验室里，每张实验台都在不同的进度上。关键失误可能直到结果出现时才被发现。

执行状态
谁停住了，谁需要帮助，哪些步骤还没有足够证据？
及时干预
在合适的节点给出克制提示，将不确定情况交给教师。
过程复盘
保留关键事件、修正与恢复路径，让评价有据可依。

眼镜协同

让提示跟上眼前的操作。

通过第一视角识别对象与动作，结合实验流程跟踪步骤状态；需要时提示目标器材，并将事件同步到平板。

01 · 流程进度

当前第 1/9 项：加水 20 µL。

眼镜跟踪实验流程，始终知道学生做到哪一项、下一项是什么。

02 · 动作拆解

调量、装枪头、吸液、排液、退枪头。

每一项拆成五个可观察的动作，逐个确认。

03 · 读取证据

读数 201.6 µL，超出标称量程。

从第一视角读出移液器上的数字，作为判断依据。

04 · 纠正提示

朝减小方向调整旋钮，目标 20 µL。

提示直接出现在视野里，学生不用停下操作去看屏幕。

学生平板

带着准备与问题，进入实验。

把器材知识、流程预习、计算草稿与问答放进同一学习任务，保留推导过程，帮助学生形成自己的判断。

  1. 01五个关卡依次完成：仪器、流程、计算、助手、实验。
  2. 02计算题来自真实反应配方，例如 50 µL 标准反应。
  3. 03学生在草稿区写下推导，提交后系统检查计算过程。

学生端原型；当前问答采用本地规则，模型服务为后续接入方向。

教师工作台

先看到需要帮助的实验台。

按求助、进度与待确认事项组织课堂信息，查看关键事件和上下文，记录教师确认、学生修正与后续处理。

  1. 01需要关注的实验台标红，例如 F 组退火温度与协议不一致。
  2. 02点开一个组，按时间查看它的关键事件。
  3. 03课后回放课堂事件，复盘过程。

教师端设计与演示数据。系统标记供教师复核，保留人工判断。

学习线索

实验前、实验中、实验后，都有连续的学习线索。

  1. 01实验前

    先形成理解与判断

    检查先修知识，理解目标与风险，预习步骤并独立完成计算推导。

  2. 02实验中

    结合状态给予支持

    识别对象和动作，跟踪流程状态，分层提示；低置信度与冲突事件升级教师。

  3. 03实验后

    用证据回看过程

    回放关键事件，复盘错误与恢复路径，用过程性评价形成下一次训练建议。

首发场景

PCR

以教师可锁定的标准流程为起点，关注多步移液和试剂匹配中的关键事件。

首版优先验证可观察事件与过程支持价值，不将最终扩增结果等同于平台效果。

  • E1试剂、样本或反应管错配
  • E2移液器型号、量程或设置不匹配
  • E3漏换枪头或换枪头证据不足
  • E4对照管缺失或错位
  • E5漏加或重复加入试剂
  • E6长时间停滞与反复求助

识别出的步骤顺序，和真实过程是否一致？

决定能否可靠发现漏加、重复加入或顺序颠倒；跳变越多，误报越多，教师越容易被打扰。

A 的顺序和真实过程一致；B 多出的每一次切换都会被当作一次步骤变化。

决定教师端事件流和课后回放能否准确定位到每一步。

A 的每一段都找对了；B 的许多碎片对不上任何一步。

LabMate AI AR glasses

Real experiments.
Visible learning.

Connect student tablets, glasses and teacher tools to support preparation, hands-on work and reflection.

See how the glasses workMeet the team

Glasses shown as an illustrative render; lens view from the BP prototype demo.

The problem

Teachers need to see the process, as well as the procedure.

Every bench moves at its own pace. A critical mistake may remain hidden until the result appears.

Execution
Who is stuck, who needs help, and which steps still lack evidence?
Intervention
Offer restrained prompts at useful moments and refer uncertainty to teachers.
Reflection
Preserve key events, corrections and recovery paths for evidence-based assessment.

Glasses integration

Guidance that follows the work in view.

Use first-person object and action recognition to track protocol states, highlight relevant equipment and synchronize events with the tablet.

01 · Protocol progress

Item 1 of 9: add 20 µL of water.

The glasses track the protocol: which item the student is on, and what comes next.

02 · Actions

Set volume, attach tip, aspirate, dispense, eject tip.

Each item breaks into five observable actions, confirmed one by one.

03 · Evidence

Reading 201.6 µL, out of range.

The pipette dial is read from the wearer’s view and used as evidence.

04 · Correction

Turn the dial down, target 20 µL.

The prompt appears in view, so students don’t stop to look at a screen.

Student tablet

Enter the lab prepared, with questions.

Keep equipment knowledge, procedure previews, calculation drafts and questions in one learning task, preserving students’ reasoning.

  1. 01Five stages in order: equipment, procedure, calculation, assistant, lab.
  2. 02Calculation tasks use the real reaction recipe, such as a 50 µL standard reaction.
  3. 03Students write out their reasoning; the process is checked on submit.

Student prototype. Current Q&A uses local rules; model services are planned.

Teacher workspace

See which bench needs attention.

Organize help requests, progress and items awaiting confirmation. Review event context and record teacher decisions, corrections and follow-up.

  1. 01Benches that need attention are flagged red, e.g. group F’s annealing temperature differs from the protocol.
  2. 02Open a group to see its key events in time order.
  3. 03Replay classroom events afterwards to review the process.

Teacher design with demonstration data. System flags support teacher review and judgment.

Learning path

A continuous learning path before, during and after the lab.

  1. 01Before the lab

    Prepare and reason

    Check prerequisites, understand objectives and risks, preview steps and work through calculations.

  2. 02During the lab

    Support in context

    Recognize objects and actions, track protocol state and layer prompts. Escalate low-confidence or conflicting events.

  3. 03After the lab

    Reflect with evidence

    Review key events, errors and recovery paths. Use process assessment to inform the next practice session.

Initial use case

PCR

Begin with a teacher-controlled protocol and key events in multi-step pipetting and reagent matching.

The first release prioritizes observable events and process support; amplification outcomes alone do not measure platform effectiveness.

  • E1Reagent, sample or tube mismatch
  • E2Pipette model, range or setting mismatch
  • E3Missed tip changes or insufficient evidence
  • E4Missing or misplaced control tubes
  • E5Omitted or duplicate additions
  • E6Prolonged stalls and repeated help requests

Recognition results

Recognition must align with the meaning of the procedure.

Connect visual perception, action sequences and protocol states so prompts have context and events can be reviewed.

Pilot path

Start with one course. Validate, then expand.

Begin with stable SOPs, observable events and teachers willing to co-design.

  1. 01

    Course co-design

    Agree on the task, learning goals and first high-value events.

  2. 02

    Controlled trial

    Define participants, scheduling and observation criteria; record the trial.

  3. 03

    Deployment & review

    Review devices, network and recording scope; assess teacher workload and process support.
